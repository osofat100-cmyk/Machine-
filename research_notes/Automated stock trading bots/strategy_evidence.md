# Which rule-based trading strategies have real out-of-sample or live evidence in US stocks/ETFs (small retail account, as of November 2026)

Legend used on every finding. Evidence type: **[BACKTEST]** = simulated on the same data the rule was found on; **[POST-PUB]** = simulated, but on data after the rule was published (out-of-sample in time, still not real money); **[LIVE]** = real-money fund/index/account records. Source quality: **[PEER]** = peer-reviewed journal; **[SPONSOR]** = research by a firm that sells a related product; **[VENDOR]** = blog/site selling tools, courses or subscriptions (low quality, treat as a lead, not proof); **[PRACT]** = independent practitioner/replication site. Research date: 2026-11-23.

## 1. Dip-buying / short-term mean reversion on index ETFs and large caps (RSI(2), IBS, "N down days", buy-the-dip on SPY/QQQ)

### Takeaway
The largest systematic test (AQR, Dec 2025: 196 buy-the-dip rules on the S&P 500, 1965 to Sep 2025, before costs) finds dip-buying does NOT beat simply holding the index; it trails on Sharpe ratio and by about 1.1%/yr in total return because it sits in cash between signals. For the very short-horizon rules (RSI(2), IBS) the evidence is almost entirely practitioner/vendor backtests with no costs or data-mining correction; I found no rigorous post-publication decay study, and the one recent 10-year run I found (2016-2026) shows the classic RSI(2) rule earning about 1.7%/yr versus 13.5%/yr for buy-and-hold.

### Cited Findings
- **[BACKTEST][SPONSOR]** AQR "Hold the Dip" (Alternative Thinking 2025 Issue 4, authors Cao, Chong, Villalon) built 196 buy-the-dip strategies on the S&P 500 (4 dip depths of -5/-10/-15/-20% x 7 dip lengths from 1 week to 1 year x 7 holding periods from 1 month to 5 years), Jan 1 1965 to Sep 30 2025; in cash when no signal. Its answer to "does it beat buy and hold?" is "The short answer is no." — [AQR PDF](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Alternative-Thinking/AQR-Alternative-Thinking---Hold-the-Dip.pdf?sc_lang=en)
- **[BACKTEST][SPONSOR]** Across all 196 variants the average Sharpe ratio was 0.04 below passive equities ("about a 16% degradation"), with over 60% of variants underperforming; on Oct 1989 to Sep 2025 total-return data the Sharpe was 0.27 worse ("a -47% degradation"). — [AQR PDF](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Alternative-Thinking/AQR-Alternative-Thinking---Hold-the-Dip.pdf?sc_lang=en)
- **[BACKTEST][SPONSOR]** On total returns, passive equities had a +1.1%/yr advantage over the average dip strategy (+0.6% vs the 75th-percentile variant, +1.7% vs the 25th-percentile variant). — [AQR PDF](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Alternative-Thinking/AQR-Alternative-Thinking---Hold-the-Dip.pdf?sc_lang=en)
- **[BACKTEST][SPONSOR]** Market-adjusted "alpha" of dip-buying averaged 0.5%/yr; only 16 of 196 variants (8%) cleared t > 1.96; none cleared the multiple-testing-adjusted hurdle of about 3.52; an equal-weight blend of all variants had a t-stat of 1.1. AQR explicitly warns that picking the best-looking variant out of ~200 is data mining. — [AQR PDF](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Alternative-Thinking/AQR-Alternative-Thinking---Hold-the-Dip.pdf?sc_lang=en)
- **[BACKTEST][SPONSOR]** AQR did not model fees or transaction costs ("such frictions would reduce the performance"), and did not test high-frequency dip rules because those are "subject to meaningful transactions costs." — [AQR PDF](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Alternative-Thinking/AQR-Alternative-Thinking---Hold-the-Dip.pdf?sc_lang=en)
- **[BACKTEST vs LIVE][SPONSOR]** AQR's explanation: dip-buying is "value investing at a momentum horizon" (it bets on reversal over weeks/months, where markets tend to trend). Dip strategies had a -0.14 average correlation to the SG Trend Index; over Jan 2000 to Sep 2025 trend-following's alpha to equities was 4.7%/yr versus 0.3%/yr for the average dip rule, and higher than even the best of 189 dip variants. The 189 dip strategies had an average market beta of 0.41. — [AQR PDF](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Alternative-Thinking/AQR-Alternative-Thinking---Hold-the-Dip.pdf?sc_lang=en)
- **[SPONSOR, secondary]** In the four worst S&P 500 drawdowns since 2000, trend-following averaged a gain while the average dip-buying variant lost money. Caveat: AQR runs trend-following products. — [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/is-trend-following-better-than-buy-the-dip/)
- **[PRACT]** CXO Advisory's review of Connors & Alvarez's "Short Term Trading Strategies That Work" (source of RSI(2)) says the published results are hypothetical backtests without transaction costs or data-mining corrections, which tends to overstate out-of-sample experience. — [CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/a-few-notes-on-short-term-trading-strategies-that-work)
- **[POST-PUB][VENDOR, backtest-platform demo]** Connors RSI(2) rule (long when RSI(2) < 5 and close > 200-day SMA; exit above 5-day SMA; mirrored shorts) on an S&P 500 price series ("SPX500/USD"), Oct 3 2016 to Oct 1 2026, 0.02% commission per side: 73 trades, 71.2% win rate, average win +1.22% vs average loss -2.10%, total return +18.1% vs +255.4% buy-and-hold, CAGR +1.7% vs +13.5%, max drawdown -21.5% vs -35%. Calendar years: 2017 +2.5%, 2018 -9.8%, 2019 -3.0%, 2020 -1.3%, 2021 +8.6%, 2022 +0.9%, 2023 -1.1%, 2024 +8.6%, 2025 +6.4%, 2026 (to Oct 1) +6.5%. Same rules on Nasdaq 100 +26.9% over the period, also behind buy-and-hold. — [Backtrex](https://backtrex.com/en/backtests/connors-rsi-2-sp-500)
- **[VENDOR]** Same site on Nasdaq 100: average win 1.58%, average loss 3.11% (loss about twice the win), strategy lagged buy-and-hold. — [Backtrex](https://backtrex.com/en/backtests/connors-rsi-2-nasdaq-100)
- **[VENDOR]** Conflicting vendor claims: one paid newsletter says RSI(2) "has survived more than 17 years out of sample" — [Alpha Algo Trading Research](https://algotr.substack.com/p/this-simple-mean-reversion-strategy); another vendor says RSI2 is "random and an artifact of data-mining" and worked in GLD's uptrend but failed in its downtrend — [PriceActionLab](https://priceactionlab.com/Blog/?p=37532). Neither presents a formal decay analysis.
- **[BACKTEST]** Internal bar strength (IBS = (close-low)/(high-low)): original study is Pagonidis (2013), daily data from each ETF's inception to 5/12/2013. A 2023 arXiv paper (Pandey & Joshi) on country ETFs, 2009-2019, long the lowest-IBS ETF and short the highest, reports strong results but the Sharpe ratios come from in-sample "best performing buckets"; for one basket Sharpe was 3.73 trading close-to-close versus -0.25 open-to-open, i.e. the edge exists only if you trade at the same close the signal is computed from; paper says "real world costs might vary." — [arXiv 2306.12434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12434)
- **[PRACT]** Jonathan Kinlay: the IBS effect only appears to work on high-volume days for US index ETFs. — [Kinlay blog](https://jonathankinlay.com/?p=2199)
- **[PEER]** Structural support for index-level mean reversion: Baltussen, van Bekkum & Da (Journal of Financial Economics 2019), 20 major indexes in 15 countries: index serial dependence was positive until the 1990s and switched to negative since the 2000s, tied to the growth of index products (futures, ETFs, index funds). — [paper](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/Indexing.pdf); [CFA Institute digest](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2019/12/dig-v49-n12-3)
- **[Academic thesis]** A 2026 University of Vaasa master's thesis on daily reversals in S&P 100 stocks finds that periods with fewer extreme events show weaker or insignificant reversal and sometimes momentum, i.e. the effect is regime-dependent. — [Vaasa thesis](https://osuva.uwasa.fi/bitstreams/dab53ba4-393d-4f3a-bca7-c012f6fcf596/download)

### Inferences
- A dip rule on an index is long the market only part of the time (AQR: average beta 0.41). Most of the profit a backtest shows is the market's own upward drift earned while invested, not timing skill. The honest benchmark is buy-and-hold (or beta-adjusted alpha), never "did it make money."
- From AQR's own numbers the implied buy-and-hold Sharpe is roughly 0.25 for 1965-2025 (price-only, 0.04 / 16%) and roughly 0.57 for 1989-2025 (total return, 0.27 / 47%). Dip rules landed below both.
- Very short-horizon dip rules (1-5 day holds) have a plausible structural basis (negative index autocorrelation since the 2000s) and a typical profile of many small wins and fewer losses about twice as large. The 2016-2026 run suggests that, on the S&P 500, the edge after publication is thin: positive, but nowhere near buy-and-hold.
- RSI(2)-type rules trade roughly 7 times a year on one index (73 trades in 10 years). The reader's test design (one unseen month fed minute by minute) would contain zero to two trades, so it cannot confirm or reject such a rule; years of unseen data are needed.
- Costs stop being the binding constraint (unlike the 1.4% crypto token). The binding constraint becomes opportunity cost: being in cash while the index drifts up.
- IBS and RSI(2) signals need execution at or very near the close; a retail bot that trades next morning's open may lose the whole effect (the arXiv open-to-open result).

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed or rigorous independent study that splits RSI(2)/IBS/"N down days" performance on SPY/QQQ into before and after publication (about 2008 for RSI(2), 2013 for IBS) with realistic costs.
- Cesar Alvarez's own year-by-year updates and QuantifiedStrategies' numbers were not retrieved (and the latter is a vendor).
- AQR did not test horizons shorter than one week, so its negative verdict does not directly cover 1-5 day rules.
- The 2016-2026 RSI(2) run is a single vendor demo on a CFD-style price series including a short side; it should be reproduced on SPY total-return data before being relied on.

## 2. Trend following and momentum (Faber 10-month MA, dual momentum, time-series momentum on ETFs, cross-sectional 12-1 momentum)

### Takeaway
Trend following has the strongest LIVE record of the families examined, but it is modest: the SG Trend Index (real funds, net of fees) earned about 4%/yr over cash since 2000 at a Sharpe of 0.30, with a 20.4% drawdown from May 2024 to May 2025. Simple long-only timing rules (Faber-style) have kept working as drawdown reducers on post-publication data but returned far less than their backtests and less than the S&P 500 in the long bull market; live tactical ETFs built on them (GMOM, Pacer Trendpilot) have lagged their benchmarks badly. US anomaly returns in general fall about 58% after publication.

### Cited Findings
- **[PEER][POST-PUB]** McLean & Pontiff (Journal of Finance 2016): the average predictor's long-short return shrinks 58% post-publication; with an estimated statistical bias of 26%, the lower bound on the pure publication effect is about 32%. — [paper](https://www.gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf)
- **[Academic]** Jacobs & Müller studied 241 anomalies in 39 markets and found a reliable post-publication decline in the US market only. — [Jacobs & Müller draft](https://www.wiwi.hu-berlin.de/en/Professorships/bwl/finance/seminars/papers-fin-acc-res-seminars/17s/jacobs.pdf)
- **[Academic]** Hou, Xue & Zhang's replication: most anomalies in some categories fail to replicate (95 of 102 liquidity-related variables insignificant), with microcaps driving much of the original results. — [NBER w23394](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23394.pdf)
- **[PEER]** Counterweight: Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen (Journal of Finance 2023), 153 factors in 93 countries: the majority of factors replicate and hold out-of-sample (about 85% replication rate in their Bayesian framework); "some out-of-sample factor decay is to be expected." — [NBER w28432](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28432/w28432.pdf); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/is-there-a-replication-crisis-in-finance/)
- **[POST-PUB]** Faber's 10-month moving-average timing (hold when month-end price is above the 10-month SMA, else cash): original 2007 paper used data through 2005; per Swedroe's summary of Faber's 2018 Journal of Portfolio Management update (1972-2005 in-sample, then the 11 years after), the Sharpe ratio rose "from 0.45 to 0.80" in the later period, with similar results for 6-, 8- and 12-month lookbacks. From a search summary only (page blocked on fetch): it is unclear whether 0.45 is the in-sample figure or the buy-and-hold figure, so treat as unverified. — [Swedroe on etf.com](https://www.etf.com/sections/index-investor-corner/swedroe-why-financial-trends-persist)
- **[POST-PUB][VENDOR]** Faber's GTAA (5 asset classes, 200-day SMA, monthly) with no re-tuning: original 1972-2005 backtest Sharpe 0.81, CAGR 11.7%, max drawdown 9.5%; out-of-sample 2006 to March 2025 CAGR 6.05%, Sharpe 0.68, max drawdown 11.7%. Simulated, by a firm that sells trading research. — [Concretum](https://concretumgroup.substack.com/p/global-tactical-asset-allocation)
- **[Academic critique]** Zakamulin concluded reported performance of moving-average timing contains substantial data-mining bias — [Mutual Fund Observer](https://www.mutualfundobserver.com/?p=4056); a study that removed look-ahead bias from Glabadanidis (2015) found the moving-average strategy at best marginally beat buy-and-hold, statistically indistinguishable — [paper PDF](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/205/Revisiting%20the%20Profitability%20of%20Market%20Timing%20with%20Moving%20Averages__1.pdf)
- **[LIVE]** Paper vs real fund: in 2011 the five-fund GTAA paper portfolio returned +2.49% while the Cambria Global Tactical ETF co-managed by Faber lost 7.29%. — [Canadian Couch Potato](https://canadiancouchpotato.com/2013/12/30/the-failed-promise-of-market-timing/)
- **[LIVE]** Cambria Global Momentum ETF (GMOM, inception Nov 4 2014, expense ratio about 0.96-1.01%): about 3.52% annualized over the past 10 years versus about 11.24% for the S&P 500 (PortfoliosLab, undated). — [PortfoliosLab](https://portfolioslab.com/tools/stock-comparison/GMOM/SPY); [Cambria fund page](https://www.cambriafunds.com/gmom)
- **[LIVE]** Pacer Trendpilot ETFs (rule-based market-timing funds; the large-cap fund PTLC launched June 11 2015): Morningstar says the large-cap fund "has tried to time the market. It hasn't gone well"; its table shows the Trendpilot US Mid Cap ETF at 4.2% vs 8.9% for its index as of May 14 2025 (return period not stated in the snippet). (From search snippet; page blocked on fetch.) — [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/funds/this-fund-followed-rules-that-was-problem)
- **[POST-PUB][PRACT]** Dual momentum (Antonacci's GEM, tested on 1974-2011, formalized 2012): ReSolve's replication found the original specification's out-of-sample performance "regressed to the median" and its wealth path gives "no reason to believe it is 'special' from a statistical sense"; results are labelled hypothetical and simulated. — [ReSolve via Advisor Perspectives](https://files.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2019/05/06/global-equity-momentum-a-craftsmans-perspective); [ReSolve thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1110908075333038081.html)
- **[PRACT]** A 2015 review noted that since 2009 dual momentum had underperformed the S&P 500 by a wide margin. — [A Wealth of Common Sense](https://awealthofcommonsense.com/2015/07/my-thoughts-on-gary-antonaccis-dual-momentum/)
- **[PEER]** Time-series momentum challenged: Huang, Li, Wang & Zhou (JFE 2020) find asset-by-asset regressions show little evidence of TSM in- or out-of-sample; a TSM strategy is profitable but performs "virtually the same" as a strategy based on historical mean returns that needs no predictability. — [WashU record](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/time-series-momentum-is-it-there/); [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/an-empirical-challenge-for-trend-following/)
- **[LIVE]** SG Trend Index (equal-weighted, net-of-fee index of the 10 largest trend-following CTAs): 4.0% annualized excess-of-cash return since 2000, 4.7% alpha to equities with t-stat 1.8, Sharpe ratio 0.30 (Jan 2000 to Sep 2025). — [AQR PDF](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Alternative-Thinking/AQR-Alternative-Thinking---Hold-the-Dip.pdf?sc_lang=en)
- **[LIVE]** SG Trend Index returned 27.4% in 2022, then fell 20.4% from May 2024 through May 2025, its second-largest drawdown since 2000; it has had 16 drawdowns greater than 10% since 2000 (one every 18-24 months). Cambridge Associates' conclusion: cyclical, not structurally broken. — [Cambridge Associates](https://publishedresearch.cambridgeassociates.com/?p=19140)
- **[LIVE]** Managed-futures ETF DBMF calendar years: 2022 +21.5%, 2023 -8.9%, 2024 +7.3%, 2025 +13.8% (Systematic Trend category: +16.9%, -3.8%, +2.3%, +3.7%). — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/DBMF/performance/)
- **[LIVE]** 2026: DBMF about +15.6% and KMLM about +15.4% year-to-date as of Nov 2 2026 — [Total Real Returns](https://totalrealreturns.com/n/KMLM,DBMF,SPY); KMLM's index was up 13.3% in Q3 2026 — [KraneShares](https://kraneshares.com/kmlm-managed-futures-etf-q3-2026-review-inflation-rate-hikes-and-the-return-of-2022/). For comparison the S&P 500 was up 6.98% year-to-date on Nov 18 2026 (7,323.63), after a 2026 closing low of 6,343.72 on March 30 — [Dow Jones via Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/dow-jones/202611188038/sp-500-falls-023-to-732363-data-talk)
- **[POST-PUB]** Cross-sectional momentum (12-1): the academic UMD factor averaged about 6.8%/yr in the years after 2009 (positive in five of six), against an average 5.5% premium over the whole post-publication period, per Swedroe (2016). — [Swedroe on etf.com](https://www.etf.com/sections/index-investor-corner/swedroe-momentum-really-dead)
- **[PRACT]** Post-crisis momentum-factor returns were largely driven by the short side (not available to a small long-only account). — [Newfound Research](https://blog.thinknewfound.com/2019/10/risk-adjusted-momentum-a-momentum-and-low-volatility-barbell/)
- **[LIVE]** Long-only momentum ETF MTUM: +2.9%/yr over the S&P 500 from 2013 inception to about 2019 — [Newfound Research](https://blog.thinknewfound.com/2019/10/risk-adjusted-momentum-a-momentum-and-low-volatility-barbell/); 391% vs 325% for SPY over the ten years to June 2026 — [24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/06/17/forget-the-plain-sp-500-the-momentum-version-captured-the-run-and-beat-it-by-15-this-year/). Alpha Architect cautions MTUM is near cap-weighted in mega-caps and is not the academic factor — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/momentum-and-size/)

### Inferences
- Realistic picture for a monthly long-only trend rule on SPY: lower return than buy-and-hold in bull markets (GMOM and Trendpilot live results), smaller drawdowns in long bear markets, whipsaw losses in fast V-shaped drops. It is a risk-reduction tool, not a way to beat SPY.
- Post-publication, GTAA kept most of its Sharpe (0.81 to 0.68) but about half its return (11.7% to 6.05% CAGR), consistent with the roughly-half decay found by McLean & Pontiff, though falling bond yields and cash rates also explain part of it.
- Diversified trend following across futures (SG Trend, DBMF, KMLM) is the only family here with a multi-decade real-money record and positive alpha, but at Sharpe about 0.3 with frequent 10%+ drawdowns. A small account can only access it by buying an ETF, not by coding a bot.
- Cross-sectional 12-1 momentum needs dozens of positions and shorting to match the academic factor; for $1,000 the only practical form is a momentum ETF, which is a buy-and-hold decision rather than a bot.
- Trend ETFs led the S&P 500 in 2026 year-to-date and in 2022, lagged in 2023-2025: performance is episodic and tied to sustained macro moves.

### Gaps
- SG Trend Index calendar-year returns for 2023, 2024, 2025 were not found; PTLC since-inception return vs the S&P 500 not found (only the mid-cap sibling).
- Allocate Smartly's own live tracking of published tactical strategies (the best independent source) is behind a paywall and was not retrieved.
- GEM/dual-momentum results for 2020-2026 not found; Chen & Zimmermann's open-source replication figures not retrieved.
- No source with the academic momentum factor (UMD) through 2025 on a consistent basis.
- PortfoliosLab's GMOM figure is undated and conflicts with Cambria's own table; treat magnitudes as approximate.

## 3. Intraday strategies (opening range breakout, intraday momentum, overnight-vs-intraday anomaly)

### Takeaway
All three are real patterns in gross data, but none has an audited live record, and the independent checks I found show the tradable edge is roughly the size of the trading costs: the ORB result reproduces gross and nets to about zero after spread and slippage; first-half-hour momentum had a negative Sharpe in a 2015-2020 re-test; the only real-money product built on the overnight effect (NightShares ETFs) closed after about a year. The published ORB/intraday-momentum results also rely on up to 4x leverage, a $25,000 account, shorting and zero or near-zero slippage, none of which a $1,000 cash account has.

### Cited Findings
- **[BACKTEST]** ORB on QQQ (Zarattini & Aziz, "Can Day Trading Really Be Profitable?", SSRN 4416622): trade in the direction of the first 5-minute candle, stop at the other side of that candle, target 10x risk or exit at the close, 1% of capital risked per trade. Jan 2016 to Feb 2023, 1,795 trades, CAGR 33.2% vs 14.9% for buy-and-hold QQQ. Assumptions: $25,000 start, up to 4x leverage, $0.0005/share commission, zero slippage and no bid-ask spread. — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/day-trading-with-an-opening-range-breakout-strategy); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4416622)
- **[BACKTEST]** Same signals: 676% on QQQ vs 1,484% on TQQQ; TQQQ was used because the 4x leverage cap often prevented the QQQ trade from taking its full 1% risk. QuantInsti's own re-test of a classic ORB version notes that removing one single Monday makes it lose money. — [QuantInsti](https://www.quantinsti.com/articles/opening-range-breakout-strategy/)
- **[Affiliation]** The papers are distributed by Concretum Group (Zarattini's firm) and by Bear Bull Traders, a day-trading community. — [Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/can-day-trading-really-be-profitable/); [Bear Bull Traders](https://members.bearbulltraders.com/can-day-trading-really-be-profitable-or-is-it-just-a-dream/)
- **[POST-PUB][PRACT; author sells MQL5 tools]** Independent replication of the unchanged ORB rule on five index CFDs, Jan 2015 to June 2026 (about 2,900 sessions each). Gross, the paper reproduces: NQ +0.131 R per trade, 23.2% hit rate, t = 2.9 (paper: +0.13 R, 24%). Net of spread and slippage: NQ +0.002 R (t = 0.0), SPX -0.081 R, Dow -0.081 R, DAX -0.038 R, FTSE -0.079 R; "no market is distinguishable from zero and four of five are negative." Costs were 6-12% of risk on every trade. Out of sample 2021-2026: "+0.06 R on NQ at best"; 2015-2017 negative in every market. Verdict: "The paper is right about the pattern and wrong about the trade." Limitation: CFD data, not ETF or futures. — [MQL5 blog (Krueger)](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)
- **[POST-PUB][PRACT, personal project]** QQQ ORB extended to 2024: consistent until 2021, predictive power "collapsed" post-2021; edge only $0.04 per trade in 2016-2023; the paper's sizing implies market orders of over 3,200 QQQ shares; with 2 cents entry and 4 cents exit slippage the profit became a net loss. — [Girlando](https://portfoliolucagirlando.odoo.com/p16)
- **[BACKTEST]** ORB on "stocks in play" (Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz 2024, SSRN 4729284, universe of about 7,000 US stocks, 2016-2023): cited total return 1,637% ([blog summary](https://letsdatascience.com/news/traders-adopt-orb-strategy-for-stocks-in-play-19394da2), which also flags single-source replication and no peer review); community recreations on QuantConnect report Sharpe about 2.2-2.4 only after changing the universe; results "depend heavily on the universe and the backtest setup"; no peer-reviewed validation. — [QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/18444/opening-range-breakout-for-stocks-in-play/); [Swiss Finance Institute record](https://ideas.repec.org/p/chf/rpseri/rp2498.html)
- **[BACKTEST]** SPY intraday momentum (Zarattini, Aziz & Barbon, "Beat the Market", SFI paper 24-97, latest version Feb 3 2025): May 2007 to April 2024, 19.6% annualized net, Sharpe 1.33, max drawdown -25%, with $0.0035/share commission, $0.001/share slippage, and position sizing that allows up to 4x leverage. CXO labels the results in-sample and dependent on assumed frictions. Placed 4th in the Quantpedia Awards 2025. — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/complex-intraday-time-series-momentum-strategy-applied-to-spy/); [SFI](https://www.sfi.ch/de/publications/n-24-97-beat-the-market-an-effective-intraday-momentum-strategy-for-s-p500-etf-spy)
- **[PEER][BACKTEST]** Gao, Han, Li & Zhou (JFE 2018): on S&P 500 ETF data 1993-2013, the first half-hour return (measured from the prior close) predicts the last half-hour return. — [WashU record](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/market-intraday-momentum/)
- **[PEER]** Baltussen, Da, Lammers & Martens extend market intraday momentum to 1974-2020 across asset classes and link it to gamma-hedging demand. — [paper](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- **[POST-PUB][PRACT]** QuantConnect recreation of the Gao et al. rule on SPY, IWM, IYR, Jan 2015 to Aug 2020: strategy Sharpe -0.628 vs 0.582 for the S&P 500 benchmark; positive only in the Feb-Mar 2020 crash (Sharpe 1.452). Original paper's average annual returns were 6.67% (SPY), 11.72% (IWM), 24.22% (IYR). Conclusion: the pattern "produces lower returns over our testing period." — [QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/15348/intraday-etf-momentum/p1)
- **[Data]** Overnight vs intraday: over 30 years of SPY, $1 held only open-to-close grew to $1.20, while $1 held only close-to-open grew to $17.27. The authors (Elm Wealth, 2025) do not advocate trading it: transaction costs including market impact "would erase most or all of the expected gain." — [Elm Wealth](https://elmwealth.com/night-shift/)
- **[LIVE]** NightShares launched ETFs in 2022 to capture the overnight effect and closed them about a year later; both funds significantly underperformed the theoretical returns even after allowing for fees; twice-daily full turnover is the suspected cause. — [Elm Wealth](https://elmwealth.com/night-shift/); [Two Quants](https://twoquants.substack.com/p/trading-the-night-effect)
- **[PRACT]** Alpha Architect (2020, SPY 1993-2020), article titled "Trading costs wipe out the overnight return anomaly": statistical robustness is low and the anomaly "is more of a random walk than it is a repeatable trading strategy." (Page blocked on fetch; from search summary, which also says outperformance held gross and net, in tension with the title.) — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/trading-costs-wipe-out-the-overnight-return-anomaly/)

### Inferences
- The ORB edge is about 0.13 of the opening 5-minute range per trade gross, and independent cost estimates are 6-12% of that same range. Edge and cost are the same size, which is why net results cluster at zero. This is the same failure mode as the reader's crypto bots, just at a far smaller scale.
- The headline CAGRs (33%, 19.6%) are produced by 4x leverage and compounding on a thin per-trade edge. Without leverage (a $1,000 cash account) the gross return would be a fraction of that before costs.
- Authors of the ORB and "Beat the Market" papers are affiliated with trading businesses and the papers are working papers, not peer-reviewed; the peer-reviewed intraday momentum result (Gao et al.) did not hold up in the one post-sample re-test found.
- Intraday strategies do suit the reader's test design (many trades per month, so one unseen month has real information), but the evidence says the expected net result is about zero.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed independent replication of the Zarattini ORB or SPY intraday momentum papers, and no audited live track record, was found.
- The authors' own post-2024 out-of-sample updates (if any) were not found.
- No formal post-2018 academic study of decay in the Gao et al. first/last half-hour effect was found; the QuantConnect re-test is a single practitioner backtest.
- The MQL5 replication uses index CFDs; a commission-only futures or ETF replication with real quotes was not found.
- NightShares' exact fund returns and closure date were not retrieved.

## 4. Evidence on retail day traders and retail algo traders

### Takeaway
Every large dataset with complete account records says the same thing: the great majority of individuals who day trade lose money net of costs, losses do not shrink with experience, and the profitable fraction that persists is around 1-3%. Where regulators separated algorithmic from manual traders (India), the profits went to large institutional algorithm users, not to individuals.

### Cited Findings
- **[Working paper, regulator data]** Brazil (Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti, "Day Trading for a Living?"): all 19,646 individuals who began day trading mini-Ibovespa futures in 2013-2015. Share with positive net profit by persistence: 1 day 29.8%; 2-50 days 15.5%; 51-100 days 8.9%; 101-200 days 6.8%; 201-300 days 5.4%; more than 300 days (1,551 people) 3.0%. Of those 1,551, 97% lost money, 17 (1.1%) earned more than the minimum wage (US$16/day) and 8 (0.5%) more than a bank teller's starting salary (US$54/day); no evidence of learning. — [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/retail-day-trading-is-an-uphill-battle/); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101)
- **[PEER]** Taiwan (Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean, Journal of Financial Markets 2014; complete exchange records 1992-2006): less than 1% of day traders can predictably and reliably earn positive abnormal returns net of fees (about 1,000 people in an average year); aggregate day-trader performance net of fees was negative in each of the 15 years. — [RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v18y2014icp1-24.html); [working paper](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)
- **[Academic]** Taiwan, learning: experienced day traders with histories of losses keep trading at nearly the same rate as profitable ones. — [Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean working paper](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/day%20trading%20and%20learning%20110217.pdf)
- **[Regulator]** India, SEBI (July 2024): more than 70% of individual intraday traders in the equity cash segment lost money in FY2022-23 (average loss Rs 5,371); loss-makers spent an additional 57% of their trading losses on trading costs. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/over-70-intra-day-traders-incur-losses-during-fy23-reveals-sebi-study-124072401110_1.html)
- **[Regulator]** India, SEBI equity derivatives: 89% of individual traders lost in FY22 (average Rs 1.1 lakh); over April 2021 to March 2024 aggregate individual losses exceeded Rs 1.8 lakh crore and only 7.2% of individual traders made a profit over the three years; "most of the profits were generated by larger entities that used trading algorithms." — [News On AIR (Govt of India)](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/sebi-study-reveals-individual-traders-face-severe-losses-in-equity-fo-segment)
- **[PEER]** US (Barber & Odean, Journal of Finance 2000; 66,465 discount-brokerage households, 1991-1996): the households that traded most earned 11.4%/yr while the market returned 17.9%; cost and frequency of trading, not stock selection, explain the gap. — [Odean, Berkeley](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/returns/returns.html)
- **[Academic]** US, Robinhood users (Barber, Huang, Odean & Schwarz; May 2018 to Aug 2020): the top stocks bought each day had average 20-day abnormal returns of -4.7%; the most extreme herding events reversed about 9%. — [UC Davis](https://gsm.ucdavis.edu/news/what-effect-brokerage-robinhood-having-stock-market); [paper](https://iimb.ac.in/sites/default/files/inline-files/attention-induced-trading.pdf)
- **[Regulator]** EU: ESMA's 2018 CFD measures cite national regulators' findings that 74-89% of retail CFD accounts lose money, with average losses of EUR 1,600 to 29,000 per client; brokers must now publish their own loss percentage. — [ESMA decision, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec/2018/796/oj/eng); [CMS law summary](https://cms.law/en/gbr/regulatory-news/esma-fca-measures-on-the-provision-of-contracts-for-differences-and-binary-options-to-retail-investors-in-the-eu). Third-party aggregators put the current average across 49 brokers at about 71% of retail CFD/forex accounts losing — [BrokerRank](https://brokerrank.net/research/retail-loss-rates) (commercial site, unverified)
- **[Algo-specific]** Quantopian's crowd of retail/independent quant algorithms: for 888 algorithms with 6-12 months of true out-of-sample data (June 2015 to Feb 2016), backtest Sharpe explained only 1-2% of out-of-sample performance (R-squared 0.01-0.02). — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/quantopians-academic-paper-about-in-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-trading-alg/); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/in-sample-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-888-trading-strategies)
- **[Regulatory change, 2026]** The US pattern-day-trader rule ($25,000 minimum, day-trade counting) was replaced by intraday margin standards: SEC approval of SR-FINRA-2025-017 in mid-April 2026, effective June 4 2026, with brokers allowed to phase in until October 20 2027. — [FINRA Regulatory Notice 26-10](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/26-10)

### Inferences
- The base rate for "I will day trade profitably" is roughly 1-3% for persistent traders. A bot does not change the base rate by itself: Quantopian's evidence shows retail-built algorithms with good backtests had essentially unpredictable live results.
- Persistence makes outcomes worse, not better (Brazil table): the more days traded, the smaller the share in profit, consistent with a negative expected value per trade compounding over time.
- With the PDT barrier removed in June 2026, small US accounts can now day trade far more freely; given the loss statistics this removes a protection rather than adding an opportunity.

### Gaps
- No US regulator or broker publishes profitability rates for retail stock day traders comparable to the EU CFD disclosures or the SEBI studies; none was found through 2026.
- No study isolating the outcomes of individual retail algorithmic traders in US stocks was found (Quantopian is the closest, and it ended in 2020).
- The widely quoted "93% of Indian F&O traders lose" figure was not confirmed; the sourced figures are 89% (FY22) and 7.2% profitable over FY22-FY24.
- US retail options-loss studies (e.g. Bryzgalova, Pavlova & Sikorskaya) were not retrieved.

## 5. Why the stock market differs from a 5-month-old crypto token for this kind of bot

### Takeaway
Three things genuinely differ: trading cost is about two orders of magnitude lower (SPY spread about half a basis point vs a 1.4% round trip), there are decades of data instead of five months, and the asset itself drifts upward so that doing nothing is a strong competitor. One thing does not differ: choosing settings on past data still overstates future results, and the more variants tested the worse it gets.

### Cited Findings
- **[Issuer data]** SPY's 30-day average bid-ask spread was about 0.0051% (roughly half a basis point), and stayed below 1 basis point in volatile periods, per State Street (the issuer). — [State Street](https://www.ssga.com/us/en/individual/insights/comparing-spy-and-spym-two-state-street-etfs-for-s-p-500-exposure); [State Street liquidity note](https://ssga.com/ch/de_ch/intermediary/insights/spy-liquidity-flexibility-to-navigate-any-market)
- **[Regulator research, 2013 data]** Small-cap stocks are very different: about 41% had mean quoted spreads of 1.5 cents or less while 25% had spreads of 15 cents or more, with much lower volumes than mid caps. — [SEC staff paper](https://www.sec.gov/marketstructure/research/small_cap_liquidity.pdf)
- **[PEER]** Standard effective-spread measures overstate true cost by 13-18% for S&P 500 stocks and by up to 97% for low-priced stocks, so cost estimates for cheap stocks are especially unreliable. — [Hagströmer, JFE 2021](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:142:y:2021:i:1:p:314-337)
- **[History length]** Daily S&P 500 tests run from 1965 (AQR, 60 years); SPY intraday tests from 1993 (Gao et al.) and 1-minute SPY data from May 2007 (Zarattini et al.). — [AQR PDF](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Alternative-Thinking/AQR-Alternative-Thinking---Hold-the-Dip.pdf?sc_lang=en); [WashU record](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/market-intraday-momentum/); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/complex-intraday-time-series-momentum-strategy-applied-to-spy/)
- **[Upward drift]** "Buy the Dip strategies will almost certainly lag buy-and-hold over the long term due to the simple fact that the latter is always invested (i.e., always earning the equity risk premium)." — [AQR PDF](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Alternative-Thinking/AQR-Alternative-Thinking---Hold-the-Dip.pdf?sc_lang=en)
- **[Upward drift, example]** S&P 500 buy-and-hold over Oct 2016 to Oct 2026: +255.4% (13.5%/yr), the hurdle an active rule on the index had to beat. — [Backtrex](https://backtrex.com/en/backtests/connors-rsi-2-sp-500)
- **[PEER][Survivorship / skew in single stocks]** Bessembinder (JFE 2018): the majority of US common stocks since 1926 had lifetime buy-and-hold returns below one-month Treasury bills (about 58%); all net wealth creation above T-bills from 1926-2016 came from 1,092 companies, about 4.3% of nearly 26,000 stocks; 86 stocks produced half of it. — [ASU W. P. Carey](https://news.wpcarey.asu.edu/20180917-do-stocks-outperform-treasury-bills); [JFE record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:129:y:2018:i:3:p:440-457)
- **[PEER]** Many published stock anomalies are driven by microcaps and fail to replicate once those are down-weighted. — [Hou, Xue & Zhang, NBER w23394](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23394.pdf)
- **[Overfitting persists with more data]** Quantopian, 888 algorithms: in-sample Sharpe had R-squared of 0.01-0.02 with out-of-sample results; risk measures carried over far better (volatility R-squared 0.67, max drawdown 0.34); the best single predictor was the Sharpe of the last in-sample year. — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/quantopians-academic-paper-about-in-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-trading-alg/)
- **[PEER]** Bank-sold rule-based strategies (Suhonen, Lennkh & Perez, Journal of Portfolio Management 2017; 215 strategies): median 73% fall in Sharpe from backtest to live (median 1.20 backtested vs 0.31 live); only 18 of 215 (8.4%) had a live Sharpe at or above the backtest; after controlling for fixed effects, the most complex strategies suffered a Sharpe haircut "more than 30%" worse than simpler ones. — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/looking-at-alternatives-avoid-complexity-and-magical-backtests/); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/live-performance-of-alternative-beta-products/)
- **[PEER]** Because so many strategies have been tested, a new finding should clear a t-statistic of about 3.0 rather than 2.0, and "most claimed research findings in financial economics are likely false" (Harvey, Liu & Zhu, RFS 2016). — [NBER w20592](https://nber.org/papers/w20592)
- **[PEER]** Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu define the "probability of backtest overfitting" and a cross-validation method (CSCV) to estimate it. — [paper](https://escholarship.org/content/qt4w1110bb/qt4w1110bb.pdf)

### Inferences
- Cost ratio: 1.4% round trip on the token vs roughly 0.005-0.01% to cross the SPY spread once in and once out, i.e. on the order of 140-280 times cheaper. A dip-and-spike bot that needed moves larger than 1.4% just to break even needs almost nothing on SPY, so cost stops being the reason for failure.
- Sample size: five months of a token is one market regime. SPY alone offers over 30 years of daily data and nearly 20 years of minute data, across several crashes and recoveries, and there are thousands of other tickers. That makes genuine out-of-sample testing (years, not one month) possible.
- But more data does not cure selection bias: with 196 variants tested, AQR needed t > 3.5 to call anything real and none passed. Every extra variant the reader tries raises the bar.
- The benchmark changes. On a token with no reliable drift, "made money" is a fair test. On SPY (about 13.5%/yr over the last decade) a bot that is in cash half the time must earn the missed drift back through timing, which the evidence in section 1 says it usually does not.
- Single stocks reintroduce the token's problems: wider spreads in small caps, most individual stocks underperforming T-bills over their lives, and backtests on today's index members silently excluding the ones that died. Dip-buying a single falling stock is a different bet from dip-buying an index.
- Risk statistics (volatility, drawdown) transfer from backtest to live far better than return statistics. A backtest is a decent guide to how bad it can get and a poor guide to how much it will make.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-issuer) recent median spread figure for SPY/QQQ and no recent small-cap spread data in basis points were found; the SEC small-cap data is from 2013.
- No source quantifying survivorship bias specifically in retail backtests of dip-buying on current S&P 500 members was found.
- What a $1,000 retail order actually pays (price improvement vs quoted spread under payment for order flow) was not researched.

## 6. Realistic expectations: out-of-sample Sharpe ratios and returns, and how often strategies beat buy-and-hold SPY after costs and taxes

### Takeaway
The strategies that survive deliver out-of-sample Sharpe ratios of roughly 0.3 to 0.7, about half or less of their backtests, which is at or below what simply holding the S&P 500 has delivered; beating SPY after costs and taxes is the exception even for professionals (79% of active US large-cap funds trailed the S&P 500 in 2025). Short-term trading profits are also taxed as ordinary income, while buy-and-hold gains held over a year get the lower long-term rates.

### Cited Findings
- **[LIVE]** Trend following, real money, net of fees: SG Trend Index Sharpe 0.30 and 4.0%/yr over cash, Jan 2000 to Sep 2025. — [AQR PDF](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Alternative-Thinking/AQR-Alternative-Thinking---Hold-the-Dip.pdf?sc_lang=en)
- **[POST-PUB][VENDOR]** Faber GTAA after publication (2006 to Mar 2025): Sharpe 0.68, CAGR 6.05% vs backtest Sharpe 0.81, CAGR 11.7%. — [Concretum](https://concretumgroup.substack.com/p/global-tactical-asset-allocation)
- **[LIVE][PEER]** 215 rule-based bank strategies: median live Sharpe 0.31 vs 1.20 backtested (-73%); 8.4% matched or beat their backtest. — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/live-performance-of-alternative-beta-products/)
- **[PEER]** Published stock-market predictors lose 58% of their return after publication in the US. — [McLean & Pontiff](https://www.gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf)
- **[BACKTEST][SPONSOR]** Buy-the-dip on the S&P 500: Sharpe 16% below buy-and-hold (1965-2025) and 47% below (1989-2025), before costs; more than 60% of variants underperformed. — [AQR PDF](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Alternative-Thinking/AQR-Alternative-Thinking---Hold-the-Dip.pdf?sc_lang=en)
- **[POST-PUB][VENDOR]** RSI(2) on the S&P 500, 2016-2026: 1.7%/yr vs 13.5%/yr buy-and-hold. — [Backtrex](https://backtrex.com/en/backtests/connors-rsi-2-sp-500)
- **[POST-PUB][PRACT]** ORB: about zero net of costs across five indices, 2015-2026. — [MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)
- **[LIVE]** Tactical trend ETFs vs benchmark: GMOM about 3.5%/yr vs about 11.2%/yr for the S&P 500 over ten years; Pacer Trendpilot Mid Cap 4.2% vs 8.9% for its index. — [PortfoliosLab](https://portfolioslab.com/tools/stock-comparison/GMOM/SPY); [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/funds/this-fund-followed-rules-that-was-problem)
- **[LIVE]** Professionals: 79% of active US large-cap funds underperformed the S&P 500 in 2025 (65% in 2024) — [InvestmentNews on SPIVA year-end 2025](https://www.investmentnews.com/equities/active-managers-stumble-again-in-2025-as-large-caps-dominate/265541); 89.5% underperformed over 15 years (earlier SPIVA edition) — [Ritholtz](https://ritholtz.com/2025/05/the-data-on-active-large-cap-underperformance)
- **[LIVE]** Individuals: 3% of persistent Brazilian day traders profitable; under 1% of Taiwanese day traders reliably profitable; most-active US households earned 11.4% vs 17.9% for the market. — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/retail-day-trading-is-an-uphill-battle/); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v18y2014icp1-24.html); [Odean](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/returns/returns.html)
- **[Tax]** US: gains on assets held one year or less are short-term and taxed as ordinary income at graduated rates; long-term gains are taxed at 0%, 15% or 20% (0% up to $48,350 of taxable income for a single filer, 2025 thresholds); net capital losses are deductible against other income only up to $3,000 per year, the rest carried forward. — [IRS Topic 409](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409)

### Inferences
- A sensible prior for any simple rule the reader finds: expect the live Sharpe to be about half the honestly out-of-sample backtest Sharpe, and a quarter to a third of an optimized one. If the backtest Sharpe is below about 1, the live result is unlikely to be distinguishable from buy-and-hold or from zero.
- Buy-and-hold S&P 500 has itself delivered a Sharpe of roughly 0.25-0.6 depending on period (derived from AQR's figures). None of the surviving timing rules reliably exceeds that; the ones with live evidence (trend) offer diversification and drawdown control instead.
- "How often do they beat SPY after costs and taxes?" In the evidence gathered: dip-buying, under 40% of variants even before costs; long-only trend timing ETFs, no over the last decade; intraday rules, no after costs; diversified managed futures, only in specific years (2022, 2026 year-to-date).
- Taxes matter little in dollars at $1,000, but structurally a bot's gains are all short-term, so at scale it must beat buy-and-hold by the difference between ordinary and long-term rates as well.
- The realistic value of building the stock bot is learning and risk control, not excess return; the evidence does not support expecting to out-earn a fractional share of SPY.

### Gaps
- The SPIVA year-end 2025 15- and 20-year large-cap figures were not retrieved from the primary report (S&P page blocked).
- No study was found that computes after-tax returns of these specific rules versus buy-and-hold.
- No consolidated, independent table of live Sharpe ratios for RSI(2)/IBS-style rules exists in the sources found.

## 7. Which of these are practical for a $1,000 account (fractional shares, few trades, cash account)

### Takeaway
At $1,000 the practical options are the low-turnover ones: holding SPY or a managed-futures/momentum ETF, a monthly trend rule on one or two ETFs, or an end-of-day dip rule that trades a few times a year. Intraday strategies are now legally open to small accounts (the $25,000 pattern-day-trader rule ended June 4 2026) but the published versions need leverage and shorting that a $1,000 cash account does not have, and their net edge is about zero anyway.

### Cited Findings
- **[Regulation]** FINRA's new intraday margin standards replace the day-trade count and the $25,000 pattern-day-trader minimum, effective June 4 2026; firms may phase in until October 20 2027, so individual brokers may still apply the old rule for a while. The rule covers margin accounts; the notice does not address cash accounts. — [FINRA Regulatory Notice 26-10](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/26-10)
- **[Regulation]** Under the new rule a 90-day freeze on new short positions or debit balances can apply to customers who repeatedly fail to meet intraday margin deficits, with an exception for small deficits (the lesser of 5% of account equity or $1,000). — [FINRA Regulatory Notice 26-10](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/26-10)
- **[Broker reading, not verified against rule text]** The minimum equity for day trading in a margin account is now $2,000, the same as any margin account. — [E*TRADE](https://us.etrade.com/knowledge/library/margin/pattern-day-trading-rule-change)
- **[Settlement]** US stock trades settle T+1 since May 28 2024. In a cash account, buying with unsettled sale proceeds and selling that position before the proceeds settle is a good faith violation; repeated violations in 12 months bring a 90-day restriction to settled-cash-only trading (thresholds vary by broker). — [Schwab](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/avoid-these-violations-when-trading-cash); [Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/trading/faqs-trading-restrictions); [Firstrade](https://help.en-us.firstrade.com/article/274-what-is-good-faith-violation-gfv)
- **[Broker API]** Alpaca supports fractional shares by API from $1 notional on thousands of US stocks and ETFs, for market, limit, stop and stop-limit orders with time-in-force = Day only; short selling fractional shares is not supported; eligible securities are exchange-listed, over $1, with average daily volume over 10,000 shares. — [Alpaca docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/fractional-trading); [Alpaca support](https://alpaca.markets/support/fractional-shares); [Alpaca $1 minimum](https://alpaca.markets/support/can-we-submit-orders-smaller-than-1-usd-in-notional-value)
- **[Scale mismatch]** The ORB paper assumes a $25,000 account, up to 4x leverage and orders of thousands of QQQ shares; the SPY intraday momentum paper also sizes up to 4x leverage. — [CXO Advisory (ORB)](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/day-trading-with-an-opening-range-breakout-strategy); [CXO Advisory (SPY)](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/complex-intraday-time-series-momentum-strategy-applied-to-spy/)
- **[Fund costs]** Ready-made rule-based ETFs carry fees: GMOM about 0.96-1.01%; managed-futures ETFs DBMF 0.85%, KMLM 0.90%, CTA 0.76%; SPY 0.09%. — [PortfoliosLab](https://portfolioslab.com/tools/stock-comparison/GMOM/SPY); [24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/04/10/the-3-managed-futures-etfs-that-made-money-while-the-sp-500-crashed/)

### Inferences
- Practicality ranking for $1,000 in a cash account with fractional shares:
  1. Buy and hold SPY (or split with a managed-futures ETF): one trade, no bot needed, and the benchmark everything else failed to beat.
  2. Monthly trend rule (Faber 10-month average) on SPY: a handful of trades a year, trivially automatable, no settlement issues; expect lower return than holding but smaller deep drawdowns.
  3. End-of-day dip rule (RSI(2)/IBS style) on SPY or QQQ: about 5-10 round trips a year, long-only, holds of several days so T+1 settlement is rarely a problem; needs an order near the close; expect a small positive return well below buy-and-hold.
  4. Intraday ORB / intraday momentum: possible in a cash account only long-only, unleveraged, and with each dollar usable for one round trip per day (proceeds are unsettled until the next day); the published edge depends on leverage and shorts and nets to about zero.
  5. Overnight-only strategy: buying every close and selling every open reuses unsettled proceeds daily, which in a cash account would trigger good faith violations unless only half the money is used each day; the only fund that tried it closed.
  6. Cross-sectional stock momentum or "stocks in play": needs many simultaneous positions, shorting and a market-wide data feed; not practical at $1,000 other than through a momentum ETF.
- With $1,000 the account is below the $2,000 margin minimum, so the new intraday margin rule mostly does not help: the account is effectively cash-only, with no leverage and no shorting.
- Dollar stakes are tiny (a 1% edge on $1,000 is $10), so data-feed or platform subscription fees can exceed any plausible profit; free broker APIs and free daily data are the only setups that make economic sense.

### Gaps
- The $2,000 minimum under the new rule comes from a broker explainer; I could not confirm it in FINRA's rule text, and broker-by-broker implementation dates (allowed until Oct 2027) were not checked.
- Whether fractional orders can be executed at the closing auction (needed for end-of-day dip rules) and whether specific brokers treat fractional shares differently for settlement was not confirmed.
- Per-order minimum commissions and data-feed costs at specific brokers were not researched.
