> Background research only. This is not legal, tax or investment advice. Rules, broker policies and tax law change; anything the reader acts on should be checked against the primary source or with a qualified professional. Research date: 23 November 2026. Items I could not confirm from a primary source are flagged in each "Gaps" list. Where a fact came from a search-result snippet rather than a page I opened, it is marked "(search snippet, page not opened)".

## 1. Pattern day trader (PDT) rule, FINRA Rule 4210: status in November 2026

### Takeaway
The PDT rule is gone at the rule level: the SEC approved FINRA's replacement on 14 April 2026 and the new "intraday margin" standards took effect on 4 June 2026, removing both the four-day-trades-in-five-days count and the $25,000 minimum. What still binds a $1,000 account is the separate $2,000 minimum for any borrowing or short selling: below $2,000 a margin account can trade as often as it likes but only with its own cash (no leverage, no shorting), and individual brokers may keep the old PDT rules until as late as 20 October 2027.

### Cited Findings
- FINRA filed the rule change (SR-FINRA-2025-017) with the SEC on 29 December 2025; the SEC published notice on 14 January 2026 (91 FR 1580) — [FINRA rule filing page](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rule-filings/sr-finra-2025-017) (dates from a search snippet; page not opened)
- The SEC approved it on an accelerated basis, as modified by Amendment No. 1, in Release No. 34-105226 dated 14 April 2026, published in the Federal Register on 17 April 2026 — [Federal Register, 17 Apr 2026](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-04-17/html/2026-07485.htm). (One law-firm memo dates the approval 15 April 2026; the order itself is dated 14 April — [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/sitecore/content/whlaunch/home/research/news-publications/20260423-sec-approves-amendments-to-finra-rule-4210-replacing-day-trading-margin-requirements-with-a-modernized-intraday-margin-standard), search snippet.)
- FINRA Regulatory Notice 26-10 (published 20 April 2026) set the effective date at 4 June 2026 (45 days after publication) and allows firms that "need more time" to phase in until 20 October 2027 (18 months) — [FINRA Regulatory Notice 26-10](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/26-10)
- The notice says the day-trading margin requirements are replaced "in their entirety": the day-trade count used to label someone a "pattern day trader" and the $25,000 pattern-day-trader minimum equity requirement are both eliminated — [FINRA Regulatory Notice 26-10](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/26-10)
- Amendment No. 1 replaced the originally proposed 12-month interim period: during the 45 days before the effective date all firms had to keep applying the old day-trading rules; after that, firms needing more time may phase in over 18 months from the notice — [Federal Register approval order](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-04-17/html/2026-07485.htm)
- FINRA's investor page: under the new approach there is no $25,000 minimum for day trading and no pattern-day-trader label; instead the firm checks whether equity is adequate relative to positions during the trading day. "Your firm might continue operating under the old day trading margin requirements during the transition" (through 20 October 2027) and "has the authority to impose higher requirements if they choose" — [FINRA, Understanding the New Intraday Margin Requirements](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/intraday-margin-requirements)
- The $2,000 floor remains for leverage: "You can trade in a margin account with less than $2,000 in equity, but you cannot use leverage," so "you must trade only with the cash you have available" — [FINRA investor page](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/intraday-margin-requirements)
- How the new test works: each day a customer makes a transaction that reduces the account's "intraday margin level" (e.g. a purchase or short sale), the firm computes an "intraday margin deficit" = the largest shortfall during the day between required maintenance margin and account equity. The standard maintenance figure is 25% of the market value of long margin-eligible stock, applied "throughout the entire trading day, not just at end of the day" — [FINRA RN 26-10](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/26-10); [FINRA investor page](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/intraday-margin-requirements)
- Consequences of a deficit: it must be satisfied "as promptly as possible"; if a customer has a practice of not satisfying deficits promptly and one is still unmet at the close of the fifth business day, the firm must block new short positions or debit (borrowed) balances for 90 calendar days or until the deficit is met. Deficits no larger than the lesser of 5% of equity or $1,000, or arising from extraordinary circumstances, do not count toward such a "practice" — [FINRA RN 26-10](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/26-10); [Federal Register approval order](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-04-17/html/2026-07485.htm)
- Firms may either monitor in real time and block deficit-creating trades, or compute at day's end and issue a call, or combine both — [FINRA investor page](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/intraday-margin-requirements)
- The SEC, agreeing with FINRA, stated that technological advances "do not mitigate the risk inherent in day trading" or the amplifying effect of margin; state regulators (NASAA) had argued for keeping strong day-trading rules — [Federal Register approval order](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-04-17/html/2026-07485.htm)
- Broker application (as found):
  - Robinhood: from 4 June 2026 "No more day trade restrictions or day trade calls with your Robinhood margin account"; existing PDT flags removed automatically; the $25,000 minimum no longer applies but "the $2,000 margin minimum equity requirement will still apply" — [Robinhood, Day trading](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/day-trading/) (search snippet; page returned 404 to my fetch)
  - Robinhood intraday buying power: needs a margin account, margin approval and at least $2,000 equity; gives "up to twice the buying power you get with standard margin" during regular hours only (9:30 AM–4:00 PM ET); repeatedly failing to resolve an intraday deficit can limit the account to cash-available trading for 90 calendar days; not available for retirement, custodial, managed, trust or "agentic" accounts — [Robinhood, Intraday margin](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/intraday-margin/)
  - Alpaca (API broker): lifted the PDT rule for Trading API and Broker API users on 4 June 2026; minimum equity for 4x intraday buying power lowered from $25,000 to $2,000 for leverage-enabled accounts; pre-trade checks reject orders that would create or increase a margin deficit; legacy API fields (`pattern_day_trader`, `daytrade_count`, `daytrading_buying_power`, etc.) removed by 6 July 2026 and replaced by `buying_power` — [Alpaca blog](https://alpaca.markets/blog/finra-retires-the-pdt-rule-introducing-alpacas-new-intraday-margin-framework/)
  - Alpaca under $2,000: margin trading and short selling require "$2,000 or more account equity"; accounts below that are limited to 1x buying power — [Alpaca, Margin and Short Selling](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/margin-and-short-selling). For non-leverage margin accounts Alpaca says there is no trade-count limit (you may trade as often as you choose provided equity covers the broker's intraday risk requirements), PDT-related restrictions and trade rejections no longer apply, and margin accounts help traders avoid T+1 settlement delays (paraphrased) — [Alpaca, Intraday Margin Rule for Non-Leverage Margin Accounts](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/intraday-margin-rule-for-non-leverage-margin-accounts)
  - Alpaca deficit timing: docs say an intraday margin call "must be satisfied within two business days" and the account is frozen for 90 days (no new shorts or debit balances) if still unmet at the close of the fifth business day — [Alpaca, The Intraday Margin Rule](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/the-intraday-margin-rule); the Alpaca blog instead says calls should be resolved within five business days — [Alpaca blog](https://alpaca.markets/blog/finra-retires-the-pdt-rule-introducing-alpacas-new-intraday-margin-framework/) (the two Alpaca pages differ)
  - Schwab: "starting June 8, Schwab plans to stop counting day trades and will no longer restrict accounts that would have previously been flagged as pattern day traders" — [Schwab](https://advisorservices.schwab.com/story/sec-approves-scrapping-25000-day-trader-minimum) (search snippet; Schwab pages blocked my fetch)
  - E*TRADE (5 June 2026 notice): day trades no longer counted; minimum equity changes from $25,000 to "$2,000; same as any margin account" — [E*TRADE](https://us.etrade.com/knowledge/library/margin/pattern-day-trading-rule-change) (search snippet; page returned 503)
  - Firstrade: "cash accounts are not impacted by these changes. They will continue to follow existing cash trading rules" — [Firstrade blog](https://blog.firstrade.com/blog/2026/6/3/the-complete-guide-to-the-pdt-rule-elimination-everything-investors-need-to-know-on-day-one) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- For a $1,000 account in November 2026 the old "three day trades per five days" cap no longer applies at brokers that have switched (confirmed from pages I opened: Alpaca, and Robinhood's intraday-margin page; reported in search snippets: Schwab, E*TRADE, Firstrade, TradeZero, IBKR). The practical limits are now (a) no borrowing and no short selling below $2,000 equity, and (b) the broker's own house rules.
- Because a sub-$2,000 account has no leverage, it cannot normally create an "intraday margin deficit" (it is not borrowing), so the 90-day restriction is mainly a concern for accounts above $2,000 that use intraday leverage.
- A bot written against older tutorials or older API versions may still contain PDT logic (day-trade counters, `daytrading_buying_power`); at Alpaca those fields were removed in July 2026, so old code can break.
- The reader must confirm which regime their own broker applies, since FINRA explicitly allows a firm to keep the old PDT rules until 20 October 2027 and to set stricter house rules at any time.

### Gaps
- Fidelity, Interactive Brokers and Webull: I could not open a primary page confirming their switch dates or policies. A third-party blog says IBKR has removed the $25,000 PDT minimum; treat as unconfirmed.
- Schwab and E*TRADE details come from search snippets only (pages blocked/unavailable).
- I did not open Attachment A (the new rule text) of Regulatory Notice 26-10, nor confirm directly that the $2,000 minimum in Rule 4210(b)(4) is textually unchanged; the approval order and FINRA's investor page both indicate it remains.
- Whether any broker still applies legacy PDT rules in November 2026 is not known; no list exists that I found.

## 2. Cash accounts: T+1 settlement, good-faith violations, freeriding, and how often a $1,000 account can round-trip

### Takeaway
In a cash account every dollar must be "settled" before the shares it bought are sold; with T+1 settlement, sale proceeds settle the next business day, so each dollar can fund roughly one buy-then-sell round trip per day. Selling shares bought with unsettled proceeds is a good-faith violation: three in 12 months (or one freeriding violation) brings a 90-day restriction to settled-cash-only buying. A margin account under $2,000 avoids the settlement wait without giving leverage.

### Cited Findings
- The SEC shortened the standard settlement cycle from T+2 to T+1 for most broker-dealer securities transactions, compliance date 28 May 2024 — [SEC press release 2023-29](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2023-29)
- Most securities settle on "trade date plus 1 business days (T+1)"; a Monday trade settles Tuesday — [Fidelity, Avoiding cash trading violations](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/trading-investing/trading/avoiding-cash-trading-violations)
- Regulation T (Federal Reserve) cash account rule: a broker may buy for a customer if there are sufficient funds in the account, or if it accepts in good faith the customer's commitment to "promptly make full cash payment for the security or asset before selling it" — [12 CFR 220.8](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/220.8)
- 90-day freeze: if a security is sold "without having been previously paid for in full by the customer", "the privilege of delaying payment beyond the trade date shall be withdrawn for 90 calendar days following the date of sale"; the freeze does not apply if full payment is received within the payment period and the proceeds are not withdrawn first — [12 CFR 220.8(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/220.8)
- Good-faith violation (GFV): buying a security and selling it before paying for the purchase with settled funds; only cash or proceeds of fully paid-for securities count as settled funds — [Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/trading-investing/trading/avoiding-cash-trading-violations)
- Freeriding: buying a security and paying for it with the proceeds of selling that same security; this violates Regulation T — [Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/trading-investing/trading/avoiding-cash-trading-violations). A deposit is needed to cure it; selling other securities does not — [Ally Invest freeriding disclosure](https://www.ally.com/invest/disclosures/freeriding/) (search snippet)
- Cash liquidation violation: buying a security and covering the cost by selling other fully paid securities after the purchase date — [Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/trading-investing/trading/avoiding-cash-trading-violations)
- Penalty at Fidelity: three good-faith violations, three cash liquidation violations, or one freeriding violation in a 12-month period restricts the account for 90 calendar days to buying only with settled cash already in the account — [Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/trading-investing/trading/avoiding-cash-trading-violations); [Fidelity trading restrictions FAQ](https://www.fidelity.com/trading/faqs-trading-restrictions) (search snippet)
- Fidelity's worked example implies buying with settled cash and selling the same day is not itself a violation; the violation arises when a later purchase relies on unsettled sale proceeds and is then sold before those proceeds settle — [Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/trading-investing/trading/avoiding-cash-trading-violations)
- The June 2026 PDT repeal did not change cash-account rules: "cash accounts are not impacted by these changes" — [Firstrade blog](https://blog.firstrade.com/blog/2026/6/3/the-complete-guide-to-the-pdt-rule-elimination-everything-investors-need-to-know-on-day-one) (search snippet)
- Margin account below $2,000: trading is allowed but "you must trade only with the cash you have available" — [FINRA](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/intraday-margin-requirements); at Alpaca such accounts have 1x buying power, no trade-count limit, and (paraphrasing Alpaca) margin accounts help traders avoid T+1 settlement delays — [Alpaca margin doc](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/margin-and-short-selling); [Alpaca non-leverage FAQ](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/intraday-margin-rule-for-non-leverage-margin-accounts)

### Inferences
- Round-trip arithmetic for a $1,000 cash account (my working from the rules above, not a quoted figure): a dollar of settled cash can buy once and be sold the same day with no violation; the proceeds are unsettled until the next business day. The bot may buy again with those unsettled proceeds, but must then hold that second position until the next business day or commit a good-faith violation. So the clean maximum is about one full-capital round trip per trading day (about five per week), or several smaller same-day round trips whose purchases together do not exceed the settled cash at the start of the day (for example four $250 round trips). Before 28 May 2024 (T+2) the same cash recycled only every two business days.
- A bot can stay violation-free by tracking "settled cash" itself and never selling a position funded by unsettled proceeds; brokers generally expose settled/unsettled cash in the account data, but this should be checked per broker.
- Under the post-June-2026 rules, a margin account holding under $2,000 looks like the simplest structure for a frequent-trading $1,000 bot: no day-trade count, no settlement wait, no leverage and no shorting. This rests on FINRA's investor page and Alpaca's documents; other brokers' treatment should be confirmed.
- Three violations are easy for an automated bot to rack up in a single afternoon if settlement is not modelled, and the 90-day settled-cash-only restriction then removes the ability to buy with unsettled proceeds at all.

### Gaps
- I did not find a primary source that states a numeric "round trips per day" limit; the figure above is derived.
- Robinhood's cash-account handling of unsettled funds and "instant" features, and Schwab/Fidelity "limited margin" (IRA) features, were not confirmed in this session.
- Whether every broker lets a sub-$2,000 margin account reuse sale proceeds the same day without restriction was confirmed only for Alpaca's documentation (and in general terms by FINRA).
- The length of Regulation T's "payment period" after T+1 was not confirmed (definition not shown on the page opened).

## 3. Margin and short selling: Regulation T, the $2,000 minimum, Regulation SHO, the alternative uptick rule

### Takeaway
A $1,000 account cannot borrow or short: both need at least $2,000 of equity. Above $2,000, borrowing is capped at 50% initial margin (2x overnight), brokers may liquidate without notice, and short sales need a locate, pay borrow fees on hard-to-borrow shares, carry unlimited loss potential and are price-restricted after a 10% one-day drop.

### Cited Findings
- $2,000 is the minimum equity for leveraged trading in a margin account; below it, no leverage — [FINRA](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/intraday-margin-requirements). Robinhood states the FINRA requirement as "at least $2,000 or 100% of the security's purchase price (whichever value is less)" deposited before buying on margin — [Robinhood, minimum margin](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/minimum-margin)
- Alpaca: margin trading and short selling require "$2,000 or more account equity"; margin is for equities only, not crypto — [Alpaca, Margin and Short Selling](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/margin-and-short-selling)
- Initial margin under Regulation T as applied by Alpaca: 50% for marginable securities, 100% for non-marginable; accounts of $2,000+ get 2x overnight and up to 4x intraday buying power — [Alpaca](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/margin-and-short-selling)
- Maintenance margin: FINRA's minimum is 25% of the market value of long positions; brokers set higher "house" levels (Alpaca: 30% for longs priced over $6, 50% for $2.50–$6, 100% under $2.50; shorts: greater of $5/share or 30% at $5 and above, greater of $2.50/share or 100% under $5) — [Alpaca](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/margin-and-short-selling); [FINRA](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/intraday-margin-requirements)
- Margin calls: Alpaca "may sell any securities in your account, without prior notice, to satisfy a margin call", gives no entitlement to an extension, and may change house requirements without advance notice — [Alpaca](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/the-intraday-margin-rule)
- Margin interest example: Alpaca charges 6.50% a year (5.00% "elite") on the end-of-day debit balance; positions opened and closed the same day incur no interest — [Alpaca](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/margin-and-short-selling)
- Short selling basics: "A short sale is generally the sale of a stock you do not own (or that you will borrow for delivery)"; because a stock can keep rising, losses are theoretically unlimited — [SEC, Key Points About Regulation SHO](https://www.sec.gov/investor/pubs/regsho.htm)
- Regulation SHO Rule 203(b)(1) "locate": before a short sale the broker must have reasonable grounds to believe the security can be borrowed and delivered on time, documented beforehand; Rule 200 requires orders to be marked "long", "short" or "short exempt" — [SEC Reg SHO](https://www.sec.gov/investor/pubs/regsho.htm)
- Rule 204 close-out: fails to deliver from short sales must be closed out by the start of regular trading on the settlement day after settlement date; otherwise the firm faces a pre-borrow requirement for further shorts in that stock — [SEC Reg SHO](https://www.sec.gov/investor/pubs/regsho.htm)
- Rule 201 (the "alternative uptick rule" / short-sale circuit breaker): triggered by a price decline of at least 10% in one day; a price test then restricts short sales for the rest of that day and the following day — [SEC Reg SHO](https://www.sec.gov/investor/pubs/regsho.htm)
- "Naked" short selling (shorting without arranging a borrow in time) is not automatically illegal but is abusive when used to manipulate; Rule 10b-21 targets deceptive fails — [SEC Reg SHO](https://www.sec.gov/investor/pubs/regsho.htm)
- Broker practice (Alpaca): 5,000+ easy-to-borrow stocks with $0 borrow fee; hard-to-borrow shorts need an approved locate in 100-share round lots (single-use, non-refundable fee) plus a daily fee of short market value × rate ÷ 360 — [Alpaca](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/margin-and-short-selling)
- Under the new intraday margin rule, the 90-day restriction specifically blocks creating or increasing short positions and debit balances — [FINRA RN 26-10](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/26-10)

### Inferences
- "Sell the spike" for a $1,000 account can only mean selling shares already owned; betting on a fall by shorting is unavailable below $2,000 (inverse ETFs would be a long-only substitute, not researched here).
- If a bot in a $2,000+ account shorts, it must handle Rule 201 days (short orders at or below the best bid will be rejected or repriced), unavailable locates, and borrow fees that accrue daily; 100-share round-lot locates are large relative to a small account.
- The $2,000 line is a moving target for an account near it: a losing streak that drops equity below $2,000 switches off leverage and shorting.

### Gaps
- I did not open the primary text of Regulation T § 220.12 (50% initial margin; 150% for short sales) or FINRA Rule 4210(b)(4); the 50% and $2,000 figures are cited via FINRA's investor page and broker documents.
- The SEC page I opened does not spell out that the Rule 201 trigger is measured from the prior day's close or that restricted short sales must be priced above the national best bid; those details are my recollection and unverified here.
- Robinhood, Schwab, Fidelity and IBKR shorting eligibility and fees for small accounts were not checked.

## 4. Taxes: short-term gains, the wash-sale rule, trader status and Section 475(f), 1099-B; crypto comparison

### Takeaway
Every profitable sale of stock held a year or less is taxed at ordinary income rates (10%–37% federal for 2026), and a bot that keeps re-buying the same ticker will have most of its losses disallowed and deferred by the wash-sale rule, with the broker's 1099-B catching only same-account, same-CUSIP cases. Trader tax status and the Section 475(f) election can switch the wash-sale rule off, but the bar is high and the election has a hard deadline. As of November 2026 the wash-sale rule still does not apply to crypto, though a House committee approved a bill in September 2026 that would change that.

### Cited Findings
- Holding period: property held one year or less gives short-term gain or loss; more than one year, long-term. Counting starts the day after acquisition — [IRS Publication 550 (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550)
- "Net short-term capital gains are subject to taxation as ordinary income at graduated tax rates" — [IRS Topic 409](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409) (page updated 9 Nov 2026)
- 2026 federal ordinary brackets, single filer: 10% to $12,400; 12% to $50,400; 22% to $105,700; 24% to $201,775; 32% to $256,225; 35% to $640,600; 37% above. Standard deduction $16,100 single. 2026 long-term rates: 0% up to $49,450 taxable income (single), 15% to $545,500, 20% above — [Tax Foundation, 2026 tax brackets (citing IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32)](https://taxfoundation.org/data/all/federal/2026-tax-brackets/)
- Net investment income tax: an extra 3.8% applies above modified AGI of $200,000 (single) / $250,000 (joint) and covers capital gains and income from a business of trading financial instruments — [IRS Topic 559](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc559)
- Capital loss limit: net capital losses offset other income only up to $3,000 a year ($1,500 married filing separately); the rest carries forward — [IRS Pub 550](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550); [IRS Topic 409](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409)
- "If you have a taxable capital gain, you may be required to make estimated tax payments" — [IRS Topic 409](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409)
- Wash-sale rule (IRC § 1091 as explained by the IRS): "A wash sale occurs when you sell or trade stock or securities at a loss and within 30 days before or after the sale you: (1) buy substantially identical stock or securities, (2) acquire substantially identical stock or securities in a fully taxable trade, (3) acquire a contract or option to buy substantially identical stock or securities, or (4) acquire substantially identical stock for your individual retirement arrangement (IRA) or Roth IRA." A purchase by your spouse or a corporation you control also triggers it — [IRS Pub 550](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550)
- Effect: the disallowed loss is added to the cost basis of the replacement shares ("except in (4) above", i.e. not when the replacement is bought in an IRA), which "postpones the loss deduction until the disposition of the new stock"; the holding period of the old shares carries over. IRS example: buy 100 shares for $1,000, sell for $750, re-buy within 30 days for $800 → $250 loss not deductible, new basis $1,050 — [IRS Pub 550](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550)
- Partial matching: if more or fewer shares are bought than sold, shares are matched in purchase order and only the matched portion is disallowed — [IRS Pub 550](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550)
- "Loss from a wash sale of one block of stock or securities cannot be used to reduce any gains on identical blocks sold the same day" — [IRS Pub 550](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550)
- The rule also covers options and contracts to acquire stock, and losses on short sales (same 30-day window around the closing of the short) — [IRS Pub 550](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550)
- Broker reporting: Form 1099-B box 1g shows wash-sale loss disallowed only when the shares were covered securities with the same CUSIP bought in the same account; "However, you cannot deduct a loss from a wash sale even if it is not reported on Form 1099-B." Report on Form 8949 with code "W" in column (f) and the disallowed amount in column (g) — [IRS Pub 550](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550)
- Different companies' stocks are ordinarily not "substantially identical"; the test is facts and circumstances — [IRS Pub 550](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550)
- Trader tax status: to be a trader (a business) rather than an investor you must (1) seek profit from daily market movements rather than dividends or appreciation, (2) have substantial activity, and (3) carry it on with continuity and regularity; factors include holding periods, frequency and dollar amount of trades, extent pursued for a livelihood, and time devoted. "It does not matter whether you call yourself a trader or a 'day trader'" — [IRS Topic 429](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc429) (updated 9 Nov 2026); [IRS Pub 550](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550)
- Without a mark-to-market election a trader's gains and losses are still capital, and "capital loss limitations and wash sale rules still apply"; trading expenses go on Schedule C; trading gains are not subject to self-employment tax — [IRS Topic 429](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc429)
- Section 475(f) mark-to-market election: gains and losses become ordinary (Form 4797 Part II), open positions are treated as sold at fair value on the last business day of the year, and the capital-loss limit and wash-sale rules do not apply. It must be made by the due date (without extensions) of the prior year's return, by a statement attached to that return or extension request; late elections are generally not allowed. A new taxpayer not required to file for the prior year can place the statement in its books within 2 months and 15 days of the start of the election year. Form 3115 is also required (Rev. Proc. 2025-23) — [IRS Topic 429](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc429); [IRS Pub 550](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550)
- Pub 550 (2025): "To make the mark-to-market election for 2026, you must have filed an election statement no later than the due date for your 2025 return (without regard to extensions)" — [IRS Pub 550](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550)
- Secondary commentary: there is no bright-line trade count. In Endicott (Tax Court) 1,543 trades in one year was substantial but 204 and 303 trades in other years were not — [Journal of Accountancy](https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/issues/2014/feb/20139036/) (search snippet, secondary)
- Crypto comparison: cryptocurrency is treated as property (IRS Notice 2014-21), and wash-sale rules "do not apply to digital assets"; brokers must report basis on Form 1099-DA for purchases in 2026 and after, along with gross proceeds — [CRS In Focus IF13359, 20 Nov 2026](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF13359.html)
- Pending change for crypto: H.R. 9172 (Applying Existing Tax Anti-Abuse Rules to Digital Assets Act, introduced 8 June 2026) would extend wash-sale and constructive-sale rules to digital assets — [Congress.gov, H.R. 9172](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/9172/text) (search snippet). It was folded into H.R. 10357, the Digital Asset Tax Certainty Act, which the House Ways and Means Committee approved 38–5 on 16 September 2026 — [EY Tax News](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2026-1972-ways-and-means-approves-digital-assets-tax-bill). The CRS report dated 20 November 2026 reports no action after committee — [CRS IF13359](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF13359.html)

### Inferences
- How the wash-sale rule bites a "buy the dip, sell the spike" bot on one ticker: nearly every losing sale is followed (or preceded) within 30 days by another buy of the same ticker, so the loss is disallowed and rolled into the basis of the next lot. While the bot keeps cycling, losses are deferred rather than lost; they are finally recognised when the last replacement lot is sold and the ticker is not re-bought for 31 days. The danger is at year-end: if the bot realises losses in December and re-buys within 30 days (including in January), those losses move into the next tax year, so the year's taxable gain can exceed the account's actual profit.
- A loss becomes permanently unusable if the replacement purchase happens in the reader's IRA (basis is not adjusted in that case per Pub 550), and the reader, not the broker, must catch wash sales across multiple brokers, accounts and a spouse's accounts because the 1099-B only covers same-account, same-CUSIP matches.
- With about $1,000 the dollar amounts of tax are small, but the record-keeping is not: hundreds of trades mean a long Form 8949/1099-B with wash-sale adjustments. On crypto in 2026 the same strategy has no wash-sale adjustment at all, which is a real difference from the reader's practice-mode experience (it may narrow if H.R. 10357 becomes law).
- Trader tax status is unlikely to be realistic for a $1,000 part-time bot account: the IRS factors include dollar amount of trades, livelihood and time devoted. This is my reading; no source gives an account-size threshold.
- Timing: for an existing individual taxpayer the Section 475(f) election for tax year 2026 had to be filed by the April 2026 due date of the 2025 return, so it is too late for 2026; for 2027 it would be due with the 2026 return (mid-April 2027, without extensions). This follows from the IRS rule quoted above.

- 2026 bracket figures come from Tax Foundation's table of Rev. Proc. 2025-32, not the IRS document itself. State income taxes were not researched.

A Senate bill (S. 5616) was mentioned in a search snippet but not examined.

## 5. Rules on retail algorithmic/API trading itself: registration, broker terms, manipulation, AI guidance

### Takeaway
No licence or registration is needed to automate trades in one's own account, but the anti-manipulation laws apply to a bot exactly as to a person (individual day traders have been fined for spoofing and wash trading), and brokers add their own automatic blocks and terms. Regulators have issued no specific rule for retail AI-driven trading as of late 2026; Congress members asked the SEC for its position in June 2026 after brokers began letting customers connect AI agents to accounts.

### Cited Findings
- Dealer vs trader: "The definition of 'dealer' does not include a 'trader,' that is, a person who buys and sells securities for his or her own account ... but not as part of a regular business"; "Individuals who buy and sell securities for themselves generally are considered traders and not dealers." Signs of needing dealer registration include holding yourself out as willing to buy and sell continuously, making a market/quoting both sides, dealing with the public, or handling others' money — [SEC, Guide to Broker-Dealer Registration](https://www.sec.gov/about/reports-publications/investor-publications/guide-broker-dealer-registration)
- Spoofing by an individual: in August 2020 the SEC sanctioned Nicholas Mejia Scrivener, a full-time day trader using accounts in his and his wife's names, for placing orders he did not intend to execute to create a false appearance of interest and then trading on the other side; violation of Exchange Act § 9(a)(2); disgorgement $140,250, interest $15,020, civil penalty $50,000 — [SEC administrative proceeding summary](https://www.sec.gov/enforce/34-89517-s)
- Wash trading by individuals: in September 2021 the SEC charged two individuals with wash trading meme-stock put options to collect exchange liquidity rebates; one defendant allegedly executed about 11,430 wash trades and collected $668,671; the complaint reportedly charged Exchange Act § 10(b), Rule 10b-5 and Securities Act § 17(a) — [SEC press release 2021-195](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2021-195); [SEC Litigation Release No. 25224](https://www.sec.gov/enforcement-litigation/litigation-releases/lr-25224) (search snippets; pages not opened)
- Brokers have closed or restricted accounts for manipulative trading: in a December 2025 SEC case, a trader whose accounts were restricted or closed by multiple broker-dealers opened accounts in others' names to continue — [SEC litigation release](https://www.sec.gov/enforcement-litigation/litigation-releases/lr-26445) (search snippet)
- FINRA Rule 5210 Supplementary Material .02: a "self-trade" is an unintentional interaction of orders from the same firm with no change in beneficial ownership; firms must have policies reasonably designed to review for and prevent a pattern or practice of self-trades from a single algorithm or related algorithms — [FINRA Rule 5210](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/5210)
- Broker-level blocks (Alpaca): "A wash trade occurs when a customer's two orders could potentially interact with each other"; Alpaca rejects such orders with HTTP 403. An order facing an opposite-side market or stop order is always rejected; limit against limit is rejected "if buy limit price >= sell limit price". Bracket/OCO orders and trailing stops are exceptions. The protection also applies to crypto and paper trading — [Alpaca, User Protection (docs v1.4.2)](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/v1.4.2/docs/user-protection) (older doc version; its PDT section is outdated)
- Alpaca also lists a "limit order price away sanity check" and restricts an account to closing transactions when a position is 600% larger than account equity pending review — [Alpaca, User Protection](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/v1.4.2/docs/user-protection)
- Alpaca's Trading API is positioned for retail users and algorithmic traders (its Broker API is for firms) — [Alpaca Broker API FAQ](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/broker-api-faq) (search snippet)
- AI agents at brokers: Robinhood announced "Agentic Trading" (beta, equities first) on 27 May 2026, letting third-party AI agents trade via its MCP servers from a dedicated separate account; alerts on each trade and a pause control. Its disclosure says Robinhood "does not control, supervise, monitor, recommend, or audit these AI agents" and that "Agentic trading involves significant risk, including the possible loss of your entire investment" — [Robinhood newsroom](https://robinhood.com/us/en/newsroom/robinhood-is-now-open-to-agents/) (search snippet); eToro, Public and Coinbase also let users connect agents via MCP — [Fortune, 29 Sep 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/09/29/robinhood-trading-agents-hood-openai-anthropic/) (search snippet)
- Congressional letter, 23 June 2026: Reps. Foster, Sherman, Lynch, Himes, Casten and Tlaib asked SEC Chairman Paul Atkins 13 questions about agentic trading, including whether the SEC gave any platform approval or no-action relief, what guardrails it requires (funding limits, position limits, order-size limits, trade approval, logs), whether using a third-party agent changes the broker's duties (Reg BI, supervision, best execution), and when an AI agent or its developer must register; written response requested by 31 July 2026. The letter notes brokers' disclosures say they do not supervise or audit the agents and that agents "may make errors, misinterpret instructions, act on incomplete or outdated information, and behave unexpectedly" — [Foster/Sherman letter (PDF)](https://foster.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/foster-evo.house.gov/files/evo-media-document/foster_sherman-request-for-information-re-agentic-trading-6.23.2026.pdf)
- The same letter cites the SEC's 2023 predictive data analytics conflicts proposal via the SEC's June 2025 page for file S7-12-23 and urges the SEC to "revisit the issues raised by that proposal" — [Foster/Sherman letter](https://foster.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/foster-evo.house.gov/files/evo-media-document/foster_sherman-request-for-information-re-agentic-trading-6.23.2026.pdf)
- Secondary reports: FINRA's 2026 oversight report lists AI-agent risks (unchecked autonomy, scope creep, auditability, data sensitivity) and, as of October 2026, FINRA "has no rule written for artificial intelligence"; regulators have taken a technology-neutral approach — [vdf.ai summary](https://vdf.ai/blog/finra-ai-guidance/) (search snippet, low-authority secondary)

### Inferences
- The legal test is intent and effect, not the tool: a bot that places and cancels orders to move the price, layers orders it does not mean to fill, or trades with itself to create volume would fall under the same anti-manipulation and anti-fraud provisions used against the individuals above (Exchange Act § 9(a)(2) in the spoofing case; § 10(b)/Rule 10b-5 reported for the wash-trading case). A simple dip-buying bot that only submits orders it genuinely wants filled is not doing those things.
- The realistic accidental risk for a small bot is self-crossing: having a resting sell limit while sending a buy (or the reverse) in the same symbol. At Alpaca this produces a 403 rejection rather than a violation, so the bot must handle that error; rapid place-and-cancel loops are worth avoiding because cancel-heavy patterns are what spoofing surveillance looks for.
- Running a bot for other people's money (or selling signals with discretion over accounts) is a different matter that can require adviser or broker registration; this was outside the brief and not researched.
- Using a broker's official API under its terms is the safe route; logging in with scripts/screen-scraping at brokers without a retail API may breach account terms (not verified per broker).

### Gaps
- I found no SEC or FINRA investor alert or enforcement action through November 2026 specifically about a retail customer's own AI/algorithmic trading bot. I also could not find the SEC's response (if any) to the 23 June 2026 letter.
- Broker terms on automated trading at Schwab (Trader API), Fidelity and Interactive Brokers (API rules, order-rate limits, order-to-trade ratios) were not located in primary form.
- FINRA's 2026 Regulatory Oversight Report and SEC 2026 exam priorities were not opened; statements about them come from low-authority secondary sources.
- The statutory text of Exchange Act § 9(a) and commodities-law spoofing provisions were not opened. A search summary said a 2025 final judgment in the 2021 wash-trading case enjoined violations of Exchange Act §§ 9(a)(1) and 10(b) and ordered over $1.3 million in disgorgement and penalties; I could not tie this to a specific release I opened.

## 6. Extended-hours and overnight (24/5) stock trading in 2026

### Takeaway
Overnight trading already exists for retail through off-exchange venues (mainly Blue Ocean ATS) at brokers such as Robinhood, Schwab, Interactive Brokers, Firstrade and Alpaca, and the major exchanges are scheduled to add a 9 PM–4 AM ET session on Sunday 6 December 2026, about two weeks after this research date and still described as "expected". Overnight markets are thin, usually limit-order-only, and lack the normal volatility guardrails, which matters for a bot that would otherwise trade them blindly.

### Cited Findings
- Exchange plans: the SEC approved Nasdaq's proposal to extend trading hours on 10 April 2026; NYSE filed an amended NYSE Arca rule on 12 May 2026; target launch for both is 6 December 2026, subject to remaining approvals — [Jones Day, Sept 2026](https://www.jonesday.com/de/insights/2026/09/nyse-and-nasdaq-move-to-23hour-trading-day-overnight-session-is-an-evolution-but-not-yet-a-revolution)
- Nasdaq's own notice (24 Sept 2026): the Night Session, 9:00 PM to 4:00 AM ET, "is expected to launch" on Sunday 6 December 2026, alongside the existing 4:00 AM–8:00 PM session ("23/5") — [Nasdaq Data News #2026-8](https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/tradernews.aspx?id=dn2026-8). Nasdaq's FAQ says the date is "pending SIP readiness as well as any applicable SEC rule changes" — [Nasdaq 23/5 page](https://www.nasdaq.com/23-5-trading) (search snippet)
- Bloomberg (2 Oct 2026): Nasdaq, NYSE Arca, 24X National Exchange and Cboe EDGX plan to add an overnight session from 9 PM to 4 AM New York time on 6 December — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-02/sleepless-on-wall-street-all-night-stock-exchanges-coming-soon) (search snippet)
- Earlier steps: the SEC approved 24X National Exchange in November 2024 for 23/5 trading (overnight part conditional on consolidated-feed changes); NYSE Arca received accelerated approval in February 2025 for 22-hour weekday trading; Cboe EDGX filed a near-24x5 proposal in March 2026; DTCC's NSCC targeted mid-2026 for 24x5 clearing — [Traders Magazine, July 2025](https://www.tradersmagazine.com/departments/brokerage/24-hour-u-s-equities-trading-advances/); [InvestmentNews, 27 July 2026](https://www.investmentnews.com/equities/round-the-clock-trading-is-coming-but-retail-investors-are-already-there/267554)
- Design of the exchange overnight session: trading week Sunday 9:00 PM to Friday 8:00 PM ET with a daily 8:00–9:00 PM pause; no opening auction; orders do not carry across session boundaries; trades from 9 PM to midnight carry the next calendar day's trade date; overnight trades are not last-sale eligible. Safeguards: temporary static 20% overnight price bands based on the prior session's prices, limit-order price protection, broker-level blocks, clearly-erroneous review, and corporate-action halts until 8:00 AM ET — [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/de/insights/2026/09/nyse-and-nasdaq-move-to-23hour-trading-day-overnight-session-is-an-evolution-but-not-yet-a-revolution)
- Brokers already offering overnight trading: Robinhood (24/5 since May 2023, more than 1,000 symbols); Schwab via thinkorswim (S&P 500, Nasdaq-100 stocks and 600+ ETFs); Interactive Brokers (near-24/6); Firstrade (since 7 April 2025, 1,200+ symbols via Blue Ocean). Fidelity and E*TRADE offer extended hours 4 AM–8 PM ET, not true overnight; Fidelity is reportedly developing it — [InvestmentNews, 27 July 2026](https://www.investmentnews.com/equities/round-the-clock-trading-is-coming-but-retail-investors-are-already-there/267554)
- Blue Ocean ATS runs 8 PM–4 AM ET and handles about 90% of overnight ATS volume; there is currently no official consolidated tape or overnight national best bid and offer; market orders are typically disabled; books are thinner and spreads wider; prices can move sharply on small order flow — [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/equities/round-the-clock-trading-is-coming-but-retail-investors-are-already-there/267554)
- Scale: overnight trading was under 0.11% of total share volume year-to-date in mid-2025 (record 0.14% in April 2025), per NYSE figures — [Traders Magazine](https://www.tradersmagazine.com/departments/brokerage/24-hour-u-s-equities-trading-advances/)
- Alpaca's API: overnight session 8:00 PM–4:00 AM ET Sunday–Friday evenings via Blue Ocean ATS; overnight orders are limit orders only (time-in-force `day` or `gtc`); all NMS stocks eligible subject to an `overnight_tradable` flag, no OTC; maximum margin buying power overnight is 2x; overnight halts typically last the whole session; overnight market data is a separate paid, indicative feed (trades delayed 15 minutes); a 9 PM Monday trade has a Tuesday trade date and settles Wednesday; from 6 December 2026 NMS exchanges join the overnight session with a daily 8–9 PM pause — [Alpaca, 24/5 Trading](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/245-trading)
- Limit Up-Limit Down bands apply only during regular hours, 9:30 AM–4:00 PM ET — [FINRA, Guardrails for Market Volatility](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/guardrails-market-volatility)
- Robinhood's intraday (extra) buying power applies only 9:30 AM–4:00 PM ET, not in extended-hours or 24 Hour Market sessions — [Robinhood, Intraday margin](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/intraday-margin/)

### Inferences
- For a small bot the sensible default is regular hours only: outside them the bot sees wider spreads, less depth, different (often paid or delayed) data, limit-only orders and weaker price guardrails, so a strategy tuned on regular-hours prices will not behave the same.
- Overnight trade-date conventions (a trade after 8–9 PM belongs to the next trading day) affect settlement tracking, wash-sale dating and dividend entitlement, so a bot that trades overnight needs date logic that a regular-hours bot does not.
- The 6 December 2026 launch could still slip; all primary wording found is "expected" or "pending SIP readiness".

## 7. Protections and limits: SIPC, limit-up/limit-down, market-wide circuit breakers, and halts

### Takeaway
SIPC protects up to $500,000 (including $250,000 cash) if the brokerage fails and assets are missing; it never covers trading losses and does not cover most crypto. Single-stock pauses (5 minutes after a price-band breach) and market-wide halts (at 7%, 13% and 20% S&P 500 declines) can freeze a bot's orders, and they only operate during regular hours.

### Cited Findings
- SIPC covers up to $500,000 per customer, including a $250,000 limit for cash, when a SIPC-member brokerage fails and customer cash or securities are missing — [SIPC, What SIPC Protects](https://www.sipc.org/for-investors/what-sipc-protects)
- SIPC "does not protect against the decline in value of your securities" and "does not bail out investors when the value of their stocks, bonds and other investment falls for any reason"; it also does not cover losses from bad advice, commodity futures (generally), foreign-exchange trades, or digital assets that are not "securities" under the statute, even if held at a member firm — [SIPC](https://www.sipc.org/for-investors/what-sipc-protects)
- Market-wide circuit breakers, measured on the S&P 500's fall from the prior close: Level 1 (7%) and Level 2 (13%) halt all listed-stock trading for 15 minutes if hit before 3:25 PM ET (no halt at or after 3:25 PM); Level 3 (20%) halts trading for the rest of the day at any time — [FINRA, Guardrails for Market Volatility](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/guardrails-market-volatility)
- Limit Up-Limit Down (LULD) price bands: Tier 1 (S&P 500, Russell 1000, some exchange-traded products) 5% for stocks above $3.00; Tier 2 (other listed stocks) 10% above $3.00; both 20% for $0.75–$3.00, and the lesser of $0.15 or 75% below $0.75. Bands double from 3:35 to 4:00 PM ET for Tier 1 and for Tier 2 stocks at or below $3.00 — [FINRA](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/guardrails-market-volatility)
- If a stock sits at a band (a "limit state") for 15 seconds, trading pauses for 5 minutes; the listing market can extend the pause another 5 minutes; a pause in the last 10 minutes of regular trading leads to a closing auction rather than a reopening — [FINRA](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/guardrails-market-volatility)
- LULD applies only 9:30 AM–4:00 PM ET and excludes rights, warrants and OTC stocks; exchanges may also halt or delay individual stocks around significant company news — [FINRA](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/guardrails-market-volatility)
- Short-sale circuit breaker: after a one-day price decline of at least 10%, short sales in that stock are price-restricted for the rest of that day and the next — [SEC, Regulation SHO](https://www.sec.gov/investor/pubs/regsho.htm)
- Overnight: in Alpaca's overnight session halts typically last the entire session and orders in halted stocks are accepted but held pending — [Alpaca, 24/5 Trading](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/245-trading); the planned exchange overnight session uses static 20% bands rather than LULD — [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/de/insights/2026/09/nyse-and-nasdaq-move-to-23hour-trading-day-overnight-session-is-an-evolution-but-not-yet-a-revolution)

### Inferences
- A halt is the worst moment for a naive bot: orders sit unfilled or are rejected, stop orders cannot execute, and the stock can reopen far from the last price, so a stop-loss does not guarantee an exit price. A bot should detect halted status, stop sending orders, and not assume fills.
- Low-priced stocks carry much wider bands (20% or more), so the guardrails protect least exactly where small accounts tend to trade.
- SIPC answers "what if my broker goes bust", not "what if my bot loses money"; the reader's crypto practice experience had neither protection, and stock losses from the bot's own decisions remain entirely the reader's.

- Other halt types (regulatory news halts, SEC trading suspensions) and how specific brokers' APIs report halts during regular hours were not researched in detail.
- Excess-SIPC private insurance at individual brokers was not researched.
